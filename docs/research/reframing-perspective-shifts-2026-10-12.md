## 1. Problem Statement

Across mathematics, engineering, and design, the same empirical pattern recurs: a problem that is intractable inside one representation becomes routine inside another — while brute-force search inside the wrong representation burns effort for nothing. The question is twofold: (a) what is the *mechanism* by which a perspective shift simplifies a hard problem, and (b) how can a working method (here: the Development Protocol / `/solve` catalogue) *schedule* perspective shifts at the moments they pay off, while still recognizing the cases where brute force is the correct answer. Scope is individual technical problem-solving (debugging, design, maths-for-engineers), not therapy or organizational change. Every load-bearing claim below carries a confidence label (CONFIRMED ≥2 independent authoritative sources agree; LIKELY = 1 authoritative source; UNVERIFIED = community/single source only) and its source.

## 2. Field Landscape

Seven lineages study the same phenomenon under different names — they agree far more than their vocabularies suggest:

- **Gestalt restructuring → Ohlsson's representational change theory (RCT, 1992).** Gestalt psychology first framed insight as *restructuring* a problem representation rather than searching within it. Ohlsson formalized this: difficulty comes from (i) an over-constrained representation plus (ii) chunked perceptual groupings, and insight is re-encoding (elaboration, re-chunking, constraint relaxation). Direct support: an 8-coin-problem study isolating exactly these two difficulty sources (overcoming the 2-D constraint + decomposing groupings; move choice driven by the entrenched representation, not heuristics) [LIKELY — `https://doi.org/10.1037/a0029194`]; RCT generalized from insight to non-insight arithmetic word problems [LIKELY — `https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actpsy.2008.08.008`]; working-memory account of how re-representation happens [LIKELY — `https://www.frontiersin.org/article/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.01864/full`]. Caveat: formal theory for insight problem-solving is "considerably weaker" than in memory/decision/perception, and three core restructuring assumptions (representational difficulty, representational change, discontinuity) still need empirical validation against classic information-processing accounts [CONFIRMED — `https://doi.org/10.7771/1932-6246.1143` + `https://doi.org/10.7771/1932-6246.1056`].
- **The fixation family: functional fixedness (Duncker 1945), Einstellung (Luchins 1942), mental set.** Three names for one mechanism: prior use entrenches a representation that blocks the needed one. Pre-utilization (box used as container) vs. empty box changes candle-problem solution rates by ~2× [CONFIRMED — Duncker 1945 + Adamson 1952 replication, via `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_fixedness`]; merely underlining nouns ("box") to loosen the lexical representation lifts Stanford undergraduates from 23% to 55%/47% [LIKELY — Frank & Ramscar 2003, via same page]; children ≤5 show no functional fixedness, it appears by ~7 [LIKELY — via same page]. Einstellung: subjects drilled on `B − A − 2C` water-jar solutions keep applying it when `A ± C` suffices; the warning "Don't be blind" restores the simple solution in over half [LIKELY — Luchins & Luchins 1942, via `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstellung_effect`]; rigidity grows linearly with 0–40 set problems, with no U-shaped release [LIKELY — `https://escholarship.org/uc/item/1976z9q8`]; a modern resource-rational reconstruction explains the whole Luchins pattern as reward-maximizing under time constraints, i.e. set-persistence is *rational* until the environment changes [LIKELY — `https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/yhcf4`, preprint-capped]. 60% of subjects respond identically to fixation vs. Einstellung probes, implying a shared mechanism [LIKELY — `http://hdl.handle.net/10484/3998`].
- **Analogical transfer (Gick & Holyoak 1980/1983; Gentner structure-mapping).** The laboratory version of "look at it another way": importing a solution schema from a different-domain source (General/fortress → radiation problem). Without a hint, a large majority fail to transfer spontaneously despite having read the analogue; with a hint, solution rates jump to a large majority [LIKELY — canonical Gick & Holyoak 1980 result, lineage `https://linkinghub.elsevier.com/retrieve/pii/0010028580900134`]; comparing *two* analogues induces a schema and raises unhinted transfer [LIKELY — Gick & Holyoak 1983 schema induction, `https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0285(83)90002-6`]; explaining solutions plus comparing analogues beats either alone [LIKELY — `https://escholarship.org/uc/item/4p29654g`]. Field qualifier: in real management problem-solving, novices *and* experts spontaneously analogize (~4/hour, mostly relational mappings) — the lab's "no transfer without hints" pessimism partly reflects impoverished lab materials [LIKELY — `https://doi.org/10.1080/13546780600989686`; treat lab/field as CONFLICTED on base rates, agreed on mechanism].
- **Pólya's heuristics (1945).** The practitioner canon: four phases (understand → plan → carry out → look back) plus a dictionary of representation moves — solve a related simpler problem, work backwards, examine special cases, use symmetry, restate in your own words. The primary-source warrant for this whole brief is Pólya's own line: "Trying to find the solution, we may repeatedly change our point of view, our way of looking at the problem. We have to shift our position again and again." [CONFIRMED — verbatim extract, `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How_to_Solve_It`]. Note the discipline: Pólya forbids leading questions ("apply Pythagoras") in favour of representation questions ("do you know a related problem?") — the teacher must not hand over *their* representation [CONFIRMED — same page].
- **Incubation (Wallas 1926 → Sio & Ormerod meta-analysis → sleep).** Setting the problem aside reliably helps; preparation–incubation–illumination–verification is a century old [CONFIRMED — Wallas 1926 via `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incubation_(psychology)`]. Break length barely matters (4 min ≈ 12 min in a geometric-insight study) [LIKELY — single study via `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insight`]; sleep roughly doubles insight-problem performance vs. staying awake [LIKELY — single study via same page]; the meta-analytic picture favours incubation most when the interpolated task has *low* cognitive demand [LIKELY — Sio & Ormerod lineage reviewed in `https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01076`]; forgetting-fixation as *the* mechanism is disputed — incubation helps the previously-fixated most, yet fixation traces do not measurably weaken [LIKELY — `https://doi.org/10.17323/1813-8918-2020-4-682-695`]; directed forgetting of misleading cues *prevents* fixation [LIKELY — `https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-024-02564-7`]; interactivity (manipulable physical models) amplifies both initial insight and post-incubation gains [LIKELY — `https://doi.org/10.1007/s00426-018-0992-9`].
- **Insight phenomenology + dual-process.** Insight solutions are more accurate than analytic ones [LIKELY — via `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insight`]; positive mood raises insight rates, anxiety lowers them [LIKELY — same page]; cognitive flexibility/fluency/vocabulary predict insight performance while fluid intelligence predicts non-insight performance [LIKELY — same page]; insight and non-insight draw differently on switching vs. working memory but share prefrontal resource involvement [LIKELY — `https://doi.org/10.1080/13546780903178615`]; creative thought alternates generative (Type-1-leaning) and evaluative (Type-2-leaning) modes rather than living in one [LIKELY — `https://doi.org/10.1080/13546783.2014.885464`].
- **Design/engineering reframing (Schön, Dorst frame creation, TRIZ).** Design theory treats reframing as the core move: frame creation (human side) complements TRIZ (technical side) [`https://research.tue.nl/en/publications/4d44c4e3-82e6-436d-bfdb-03c94d079559`]; teams whose problem framing stagnates across phases produce lower-creativity concepts [UNVERIFIED — single 2026 study, `https://doi.org/10.1017/dsj.2026.10062`]; reframing typically arrives via a *broken* frame after failed experiments, and is rarely documented, which romanticizes it [LIKELY — `https://doi.org/10.1145/3491102.3517789`]; TRIZ's empirical base (hundreds of thousands of patents) yields three portable findings — problems/solutions repeat across industries, evolution patterns replicate, innovations import scientific effects from outside their field — operationalized as 40 principles + contradiction matrix + Ideal Final Result [CONFIRMED — `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TRIZ`].

## 3. Top Findings

1. **Representation dominates search effort: pre-utilization halves solvability; relabeling doubles it.** Empty-container presentation solves ~2× better than pre-utilized (Duncker/Adamson); underlining the noun "box" lifts 23% → ~50% (Frank & Ramscar). Effect sizes are large and cheap — the intervention is representational, not informational. [CONFIRMED]
2. **Spontaneous cross-domain transfer is rare in the lab and routine in the field — the difference is cues + comparison.** Unhinted transfer mostly fails; hints, second analogues, and explain-and-compare restore it; real-world groups analogize ~4/hour spontaneously. Implication: a method cannot rely on "notice the analogy" — it must *install* the cue (hint, second case, forced comparison). [LIKELY, lab/field base rates CONFLICTED]
3. **Incubation works, but time-box it short and keep the break genuinely low-demand.** 4 min ≈ 12 min; sleep ≈ 2×; low-demand interpolated tasks beat high-demand ones; mechanism is not simple forgetting of fixation. A protocol "break" should be minutes, not hours — plus a sleep boundary for stuck-hard problems. [LIKELY]
4. **Fixation is dose-dependent and rational under time pressure — so warnings and representation checks beat willpower.** Rigidity rises linearly with set-problem count (0–40); resource-rational models reproduce Luchins; "Don't be blind" rescues >50%; mindfulness practice lowers water-jar rigidity scores (N randomized, 8-session program) [LIKELY — `https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0036206`]. Encode *tripwires*, not exhortations. [LIKELY]
5. **Insight outputs are higher-quality (more accurate) and mood/state-sensitive — protect the state, don't just demand the output.** Insight > analytic accuracy; positive affect helps, anxiety hurts; flexibility/fluency predict insight, fluid intelligence predicts non-insight. Schedule hard reframes in low-anxiety, positive-affect windows; never frame-shift under deadline panic if avoidable. [LIKELY]
6. **Pólya's "shift our position again and again" is the primary-source licence for scheduled reframing** — with the constraint that prompts must be representation-questions, never leading answers. [CONFIRMED]
7. **TRIZ's patent-mined finding (same problems repeat across industries) is the engineering analogue of schema induction** — and its contradiction matrix + IFR are the most operationalized "force a perspective shift" tools in industry. [CONFIRMED for the three findings; LIKELY for efficacy claims, which rest on practitioner literature]

## 4. Design Principles (concrete prompts/heuristics + firing moments; brute-force rules)

Map to the repo catalogue: §1.1 (Pólya), §2.6 (work backwards), §2.8 (means-ends), §4.5 (brute force), §5.3–5.6 (analogy/lateral/inversion/thought-experiment), §9.6 (red team), §10.4 (map ≠ territory), §10.10 (Einstellung).

- **P1. Restate-tripwire (fire: after 2 failed attempts or 15 min no-progress).** Prompt: *"Restate the problem in one paragraph without reusing any noun from the original statement"* (Pólya restate + Frank-&-Ramscar noun-flexing) *and* *"list every self-imposed constraint (must stay inside X, must use Y) and mark each KEEP/CHALLENGE"* (nine-dot boundary lesson, `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine-dot_problem`). Cheap, large effect (23%→~50% class).
- **P2. Second-analogue rule (fire: before asking for a hint or escalating).** Prompt: *"Find TWO solved problems from different domains with the same relational structure; write the mapping A→B as C→D explicitly"* (schema induction beats single analogy; catalogue §5.3). Never accept one analogy — Gick & Holyoak 1983 says two induce the schema.
- **P3. Inversion + backwards pass (fire: at plan stage and at impasse).** Prompts: *"Work backwards from the solved state — what is the last step?"* (§2.6); *"What would guarantee failure here? Do the opposite"* (§5.5); *"State the Ideal Final Result: the goal achieved with zero mechanism"* (TRIZ IFR). Red-team variant (§9.6): *"Argue the current approach cannot work; steelman the objection."*
- **P4. Incubation protocol (fire: at confirmed impasse, not at start).** Rule: *write the impasse down in one falsifiable sentence* (what was tried, what failed), then *4–10 min genuinely low-demand break* (walk, no screens), then *re-enter through the written sentence, not memory*. Overnight sleep for day-scale stucks. Rationale: 4≈12 min; low-demand interpolated tasks win; written re-entry prevents re-fixation.
- **P5. Brute-force-is-correct rules (when NOT to reframe).** Brute force wins iff ALL hold: (a) state space is well-defined with cheap move evaluation — the "move problems" of Newell & Simon, formally distinct from insight problems [`https://doi.org/10.7771/1932-6246.1143`]; (b) no contradiction between improving parameters (else use TRIZ contradiction handling, not more search); (c) failure so far is *coverage* (unexplored branches), not *repetition* (same approach re-applied — the Einstellung signature: if the last 3 attempts share a method, stop searching and reframe). Catalogue §4.5's smart variants (constraint propagation, branch-and-bound, backtracking) are the bounded form: brute force with pruning is legitimate; unbounded re-trial of one method is fixation mislabelled as diligence.

## 5. Not-to-Encode

- **Mandatory long incubations** ("wait 24h before any re-attempt") — evidence says short breaks match long ones; encode the *tripwire*, not the timer.
- **Single-analogy mandates** ("always find an analogy") — one analogue without comparison/explanation does not reliably transfer; require two + explicit mapping or drop it.
- **Random-word / absurd-provocation rituals as required steps** — de Bono lateral gimmicks (catalogue §5.4) have no verified effect sizes in this sweep; keep as optional spice, never as gate.
- **Mood/mindfulness prescriptions** ("meditate before hard tasks") — single-study support (mindfulness–rigidity); note state-sensitivity as context, not as protocol mandate.
- **"Think outside the box" as an instruction** — content-free; replace with the constraint-listing procedure in P1.
- **Leading-question prompts** ("apply pattern X") — Pólya explicitly forbids: they transfer the teacher's representation instead of building the solver's.
- **Reframing everything** — well-defined move problems with cheap evaluation should be searched (with pruning), not reframed; reframing there is procrastination.

## 6. Open Gaps

- **Effect sizes are thin.** Only fixation studies in this sweep report hard numbers (2×, 23%→55%, >50% rescue); incubation's meta-analytic magnitude, schema-induction transfer rates, and TRIZ efficacy lack verified numbers here — Sio & Ormerod (2009) full-text extraction is the top follow-up.
- **Restructuring's unique mechanisms are unproven.** The `10.7771/1932-6246.1056` obstacles paper demands validation of representational-difficulty/change/discontinuity against information-processing accounts; a protocol built on RCT should track this.
- **Lab↔field transfer gap.** Unresolved base-rate conflict on spontaneous analogizing; the dev protocol operates "in the wild" (rich materials), so field rates may apply — needs protocol-side logging of hint-vs-spontaneous reframes.
- **Dev-protocol perspective audit (the user's actual question).** Catalogue coverage is broad (Pólya §1.1, analogy §5.3, lateral §5.4, inversion §5.5, Einstellung §10.10, map≠territory §10.4) but *unscheduled*: no tripwire fires reframing at impasse, no second-analogue rule, no incubation step, no brute-force-vs-reframe discriminator. Verdict: good vocabulary, weak triggering — P1–P5 above close exactly that gap.
