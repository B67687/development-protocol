# Hiccups as Design — When Smoothness Signals Neglect, and How to Introduce Deliberate Friction

**Date:** 2026-10-12

## 1. Problem Statement

T-079 claims that **excessive smoothness in a process is itself a diagnostic signal**: if the dev protocol runs friction-free, something is being skipped — verification, dissent, or contact with reality. The proposed remedy is to **deliberately introduce hiccups** (roadblocks that force slowdown) as the balancing side of the same coin: flow needs both lubricant and grit. The hard part is dose and kind: friction that restores balance is productive; friction that merely obstructs is bureaucracy. This brief answers three questions: (a) when does smoothness indicate neglect rather than health, (b) what kinds of hiccups have causal evidence behind them, and (c) how much friction, placed where, with what guardrails — so the protocol can encode "smart hiccups" without encoding obstruction.

## 2. Field Landscape

Eight territories bear on T-079, spanning learning science, safety-critical practice, and HCI:

1. **Desirable difficulties (Bjork & Bjork)** — learning conditions that feel slow and effortful yet improve long-term retention and transfer; the founding "smooth feels good but teaches little" result.
2. **The disfluency effect and its contested replications** — the narrow claim that perceptual difficulty (hard fonts) improves memory; initial positives followed by nulls and moderator-hunting. Included as a cautionary tale.
3. **Productive failure / PS-I (Kapur)** — attempt-before-instruction beats instruction-first on conceptual understanding and transfer, with a documented non-STEM null. Covered in depth in the sibling brief `g3-learning-by-doing-2026-10-04.md`; cross-linked, not duplicated here.
4. **Dual-process theory and cognitive debiasing (Croskerry)** — fast Type 1 intuition dominates and breeds bias; the critical debiasing step is forced decoupling to slow Type 2 verification; forcing functions are the strongest intervention class.
5. **Automation complacency, automation bias, and vigilance decrements** — the direct evidence that smooth, reliable automation degrades human monitoring; constant reliability is the complacency machine.
6. **Checklists, pause-points, and mistake-proofing (Haynes/WHO, Gawande; Shingo poka-yoke)** — institutionalized hiccups with mortality/morbidity numbers attached; hard constraints vs. soft reminders.
7. **Fluency-truth / illusory truth** — the mechanism by which smoothness hides errors: fluent processing is used as a cue for truth, so repeated smooth exposure inflates belief without evidence.
8. **Friction-by-design in HCI + cognitive forcing for AI reliance (Buçinca, Vasconcelos)** — deliberate friction (decide-first, partial explanations, cost-benefit-calibrated explanations) as the current experimental home of "smart hiccups."

## 3. Top Findings

**F1. Attempt-first and retrieval friction reliably improve retention and transfer — smoothness of study is the neglect signal. [CONFIRMED]**
Post-testing beats extended reading on 7-day retention at d = 0.74, and even pre-testing (attempt before exposure) beats reading at d = 0.35, though post-testing also transfers to untested questions while pre-testing does not (`https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-019-0053-1`). A naturalistic classroom study finds one end-of-class retrieval round lifts exam scores ~10% over re-teaching (`https://doi.org/10.14349/rlp.2020.v52.3`). The Annual Review synthesis holds the testing effect robust across materials, ages, and abilities (`https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-010419-051019`). Productive-failure evidence (attempt → fail → instruction) is cross-linked from `g3-learning-by-doing-2026-10-04.md` rather than re-litigated. Reading: a loop that feels fluent because it never demands generation is the precise shape of neglect.

**F2. Fluency is used as a truth cue: repetition inflates judged truth logarithmically, mediated by processing fluency — smooth exposure manufactures belief. [CONFIRMED]**
Validity judgments rise logarithmically with repetitions (0–9 exposures), the largest jump coming at the second encounter, and processing fluency mediates the effect while skepticism only slightly moderates it (`https://doi.org/10.1080/15534510.2015.1137224`; `https://doi.org/10.1186/s41235-021-00301-5`). Epistemological analysis adds the boundary condition: fluency is a *reliable* truth cue only where most encountered statements are true — inside a curated protocol, fluency is honest; inside an unfiltered feed, it is a trap (`https://doi.org/10.1007/s13164-010-0039-7`). Extracted background: `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illusory_truth_effect`. For T-079: repeated smooth agreement (with a tool, a checklist, a teammate) is evidence of exposure, not evidence of correctness.

**F3. Constant-reliability smoothness induces complacency; variability restores monitoring. [CONFIRMED]**
In flight-simulation monitoring, variable-reliability automation yielded 81.6% failure detection vs. 32.7% under constant reliability — constant smoothness *caused* the monitoring collapse, and the gap vanished when monitoring was the only task (`https://doi.org/10.1177/154193129203601833`). The out-of-the-loop synthesis adds the full casualty list of smoothness: vigilance decrements, over-trust, situation-awareness loss, manual-skill decay — and finds intermediate levels of automation (joint human–computer control) outperform full automation (`https://doi.org/10.1002/prs.680160304`). Automation-bias field data: cancers found in 46% of unaided reads were found in only 21% when a (failing) aid was present; pilots using EICAS detected fewer malfunctions than when flying manually (extracted via sidecar from `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automation_bias`). Smoothness without probes is the neglect signature.

**F4. Vigilance decays predictably, and hiccup-like inputs — feedback, rest, target-similar signals — restore it; expertise can eliminate it. [CONFIRMED]**
Mackworth's clock-test program: feedback trials raised sensitivity and cut the decrement; amphetamine + feedback eliminated it; and introducing non-target "artificial" signals similar to targets reduced the decrement, while dissimilar signals did not — i.e., only *relevant* hiccups help (extracted via sidecar from `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigilance_(psychology)`). Trained/expert screeners and pilots show sharply reduced or absent decrements. Reading: breaks and feedback are not rewards for good monitoring; they are structural requirements of sustained monitoring.

**F5. Institutionalized hiccups — checklists and pause-points — cut deaths and complications by as much as one-third where used. [Effect CONFIRMED; magnitude LIKELY]**
The WHO Surgical Safety Checklist program reports death/complication reductions "by as much as one-third in centres where it is used" (extracted via sidecar from `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHO_Surgical_Safety_Checklist`; primary trial `Haynes et al. 2009, https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMsa0810119`). A 33-study systematic review concurs: increased hazard detection, fewer complications, better team communication (`https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjqs-2012-001797`). The load-bearing details are social, not paper: local champions, staff-feedback adaptation, leadership engagement, and local ownership predict uptake; lack of buy-in predicts failure (`https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjqs-2012-001797`; `https://doi.org/10.1186/s13037-024-00410-2`). A hiccup nobody owns becomes zombie compliance.

**F6. Cognitive forcing functions — decide-first, partial explanations, verification-cost-calibrated explanations — reduce AI overreliance. [CONFIRMED]**
Requiring an independent judgment before viewing AI advice reduces overreliance (Buçinca et al. 2021, as synthesized in `https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1967356`, which adds the mechanism: delay preserves a populated hypothesis set before the AI-supported interpretation goes focal). Partial explanations beat no explanations at cutting reliance on wrong AI suggestions (`https://doi.org/10.1145/3710946`). Five studies (N = 731) show explanations reduce overreliance exactly when they lower the *cost of verifying* the AI's prediction — task difficulty, explanation difficulty, and payoff all move reliance strategically (`https://doi.org/10.1145/3579605`). Debiasing theory names the shared mechanism: deliberate decoupling from Type 1 to Type 2, with forcing functions as the strongest intervention class (`https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjqs-2012-001712`; `https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjqs-2012-001713`).

**F7. Perceptual disfluency (hard-to-read fonts) is contested — the flagship "cosmetic hiccup" does not replicate cleanly. [CONFLICTED]**
Early demonstrations reported memory gains from disfluent fonts, but the moderator-search paper urges caution on boundary conditions (`https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/acp.3023`); direct replications fail with and without images (n = 144, n = 142; `https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.3960675`); a preregistered N = 220 multiverse replication finds nulls on retention, comprehension, and transfer across all specifications (`https://osf.io/5h8uf_v1`); and a reproducibility audit of the null-finding meta-analysis finds its recall/moderator results doubtful (`https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09579-1`). Cosmetic difficulty without deeper processing is obstruction, not productive friction.

**F8. The calibration rule: difficulty helps low-element-interactivity tasks and overloads high ones; friction must expand the hypothesis set, not merely add load. [LIKELY]**
An integrative DDF–CLT–PLT model proposes raising difficulty for low-complexity material (focus gain) while lowering it for high-complexity material (overload prevention) (`https://doi.org/10.1177/17470218241308143`). The hypothesis-cue account sharpens the design test: effective friction populates competing alternatives and prompts discriminating evidence-search; friction that only slows a single focal hypothesis breeds confirmatory search inside an impoverished set (`https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1967356`). The S2D2 framework adds the motivational gate: learners disengage when perceived effort is high *and* perceived learning is low, so hiccups must make progress visible (`https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09766-w`). Practitioner voice agrees — "the right amount of friction prevents errors, avoids unintentional actions, promotes critical thought" (`https://humanebydesign.com/garden/embrace-friction`).

**F9. Hiccups can backfire: retrieval friction increases misinformation uptake unless warnings are attached. [LIKELY]**
A forensic-context meta-analysis (26 studies, k = 72, N = 4,218) finds initial retrieval practice *increases* later misinformation acceptance at d = 0.34 — recognition formats worse (d = 0.66) than free recall (d = 0.17) — but explicit warnings erase the effect (d = −0.07) (`https://doi.org/10.11144/javeriana.upsy25.resf`). Every friction device needs its warning label: the hiccup must name what it guards against, or it becomes a new error channel.

## 4. Design Principles

1. **Decide-first gates.** No consequential step consumes assistance, precedent, or a prior conclusion before recording an independent judgment plus one rival hypothesis ("what evidence would prove this wrong"). Directly implements F6 and the hypothesis-cue mechanism; cheapest hiccup with the strongest warrant.
2. **Couple every hiccup to a feedback channel.** A pause that returns no information (no assert, no count, no contrast line) is a tollbooth, not a checkpoint. F4 (feedback restores vigilance) and F1 (feedback-gated retrieval) jointly require: predict → act → compare expected-vs-actual, in writing.
3. **Dose by element-interactivity; place hiccups at seams, never inside load peaks.** Small verification pauses between phases (the surgical "sign in / time out / sign out" shape); no novel difficulty injected mid-complex-reasoning. Implements F8's calibration rule.
4. **Make friction discriminative, not merely slow.** Each hiccup must surface an alternative, a base rate, or a disconfirmation probe — never a bare delay or re-read. Implements F2 (fluency≠truth), F7's negative lesson, and F8's hypothesis-set test.
5. **Instrument smoothness itself as a monitored metric.** Track agreement rate with assistance, time-since-last-independent-check, and probe-detection rates; treat long smooth runs without probes as suspect (F3's 81.6% vs. 32.7% is the template: vary reliability deliberately with spot-checks). Smooth *and* probed = stable; smooth *and* unprobed = neglect.

## 5. Not-to-Encode

- **Cosmetic disfluency** (hard fonts, artificial uglification, gratuitous reading difficulty) — contested warrant (F7); difficulty must force generation or comparison, not perception.
- **Delay-for-delay's-sake** — waiting periods, cooldowns, or re-approvals that reduce no verification cost and populate no alternative; Vasconcelos (F6) predicts these merely shift reliance, not improve it.
- **Checklists as unread paperwork** — mandating the form without local ownership, champion, and adaptation rights reproduces the known failure mode (F5); encode the *pause with named verifier*, not the document.
- **Attempt-first outside concept-learning terrain** — productive failure's warrant is STEM-concept-shaped with a documented non-STEM null (see `g3-learning-by-doing-2026-10-04.md` §6); do not encode "always fail first" as a universal.
- **Retrieval-gates without warnings in belief-contested contexts** — retrieval before correction raises suggestibility (F9, d = 0.34); pair with explicit warnings and free-recall (not recognition) formats.
- **Human-"monitoring" of constant-reliability automation as a safety plan** — F3 shows this is the complacency recipe; either vary the automation's demands or assign the human generative work, not passive watch.
- **Fluency or confidence as a correctness signal** — repetition-inflated agreement is exposure, not evidence (F2); never let "it went smoothly" close a gate.

## 6. Open Gaps

1. **Dose–response curves are missing.** No reviewed source states how long a pause, how many rivals, or how frequent a probe is optimal; F8's calibration rule is directional, not quantitative. Best next probe: instrument one gate (decide-first + one rival) and vary probe frequency against error-catch rate.
2. **Individual differences moderate friction's payoff.** High need-for-cognition users benefit more from explanations/forcing (`https://doi.org/10.1145/3710946`); complacency-potential scales predict monitoring attitudes (`https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.00225`). One-size hiccups will over-burden some and under-challenge others.
3. **Debiasing evidence stops at vignettes.** A 2022 systematic review (2,232 screened, 17 included) finds mixed effectiveness, zero studies in live clinical settings, and zero long-term follow-up (`https://doi.org/10.1101/2022.09.12.22279750`). All protocol transfer is extrapolation until field-tested.
4. **Habituation may erode hiccups.** CDSS familiarity produces desensitization/habituation (per `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automation_bias` extraction); no source gives a refresh schedule for forcing functions. Assume hiccups decay; rotate them.
5. **No direct studies of friction dosing in software/dev-process work.** Every claim above is imported from learning, medicine, aviation, or HCI. The dev-protocol application (T-079's actual target) is an untested transfer — run it as an experiment with pre-registered catch-metrics, not as doctrine.
