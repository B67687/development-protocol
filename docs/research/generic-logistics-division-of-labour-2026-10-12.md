# Generic Logistics vs Specific Problems — Division of Labour and Amortization

**Date:** 2026-10-12
**Question:** A large share of real project work is LOGISTICS (tooling, conventions, docs, github, readme, publishing) that is GENERAL rather than specific, and it is unfun and repetitive. How should a method separate and handle the GENERIC/logistics work vs the SPECIFIC problem work, and amortize the general solutions across projects?

## 1. Problem Statement

The user's verbatim observation: in real projects (exemplar: the ithmb codec project that inspired the dev protocol), roughly half the time goes to *logistical* problems — keeping public GitHub clean (hence the github-workflows skill), SWE documentation of the whys and hows, README format, push/publishing problems — rather than the project's *specific* technical problems. Two properties make this painful: (a) the logistics work is **general** (it recurs in every project, so solving it inside one project feels like paying a project-specific price for a cross-project good), and (b) it is **unfun and repetitive**, draining the enjoyment that the specific problem work provides.

Scope of this brief: the solo/small-team OSS-builder context first (one person, many public repos, agent-assisted), generalized second to small teams. The verdict sought is a **division-of-labour principle** (what belongs to generic-logistics vs specific-problem work, who/what handles each) plus an **amortization mechanism** (how a general solution, once built, pays for itself across projects instead of being re-solved). The fun constraint is treated as load-bearing, not decorative: following G3's motivation constraint (Senge via Kolb lineage — the learner must want the knowledge), a method that makes the work systematically unfun will be abandoned regardless of its correctness. (G3 cross-link: `docs/research/g3-learning-by-doing-2026-10-04.md`.)

## 2. Field Landscape

Seven territories bear on the question. Each is summarized with its source and confidence.

**2a. Essential vs accidental complexity (Brooks).** Fred Brooks, "No Silver Bullet — Essence and Accident in Software Engineering" (1986; IEEE Computer April 1987), distinguishes *essential* complexity (inherent in the problem — "if users want a program to do 30 different things, then those 30 things are essential") from *accidental* complexity (produced by the tools and process, and removable). His two load-bearing claims: (i) no single technology/management technique promises even an order-of-magnitude improvement within a decade; (ii) by 1986 accidental complexity had already been substantially reduced (high-level languages), so programmers spend most time on essential complexity, and shrinking remaining accident to zero cannot yield order-of-magnitude gains — only attacks on *essence* can. **[LIKELY]** (single authoritative source, full-text verified via sidecar extract of `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Silver_Bullet`; the paper's thesis is uncontested in the literature but this brief verified only one summary source, so LIKELY not CONFIRMED.) *Mapping to our question:* generic logistics ≈ accidental complexity (toolchain, publishing, hygiene), specific problems ≈ essential complexity. Brooks's warning cuts both ways: logistics is worth attacking precisely because it is accident, but no single logistics fix (one skill, one template) is a silver bullet either.

**2b. Yak shaving.** Term coined by Carlin J. Vieri (MIT PhD student, 1990s), after a Ren & Stimpy "Yak Shaving Day" episode; denotes the cascade of seemingly-unrelated prerequisite tasks before the real task can proceed. **[LIKELY]** (TechTarget definition + practitioner taxonomy agree on origin and meaning; both community-tier, mutually consistent, full-text verified: `https://www.techtarget.com/whatis/definition/yak-shaving`, `https://jessitron.com/2017/07/03/a-taxonomy-of-yak-shaving/`.) Practitioner Jess Kerr's taxonomy adds the key refinement: not all yaks are waste — *Attack Yaks* block progress, *Trim/HackHacking Yaks* speed up many future tasks, *Golden Yaks* change the way you work; the goal is "a team development flow that moves forward while getting smoother every day." **[UNVERIFIED]** (single practitioner source, no peer review — but directly on-point.) *Mapping:* the user's logistics pain is a yak stack; the taxonomy already contains the division (blockers to defeat vs golden yaks to invest in).

**2c. Toil vs overhead vs engineering (Google SRE).** The SRE book defines *toil* strictly — manual, repetitive, automatable, tactical, devoid of enduring value, scaling linearly (O(n)) with service growth — and explicitly distinguishes it from *overhead* (meetings, goals, HR paperwork: necessary, not toil) and from grungy-but-improving work (e.g., cleaning up alert config, which produces permanent improvement and is therefore *not* toil). Policy: cap toil at 50% of SRE time, ≥50% on engineering that reduces future toil; quarterly surveys average ≈33% toil. **[LIKELY]** (single authoritative source — Google SRE book, full-text verified: `https://sre.google/sre-book/eliminating-toil/`; figures are Google-internal surveys, not independently replicated.) *Mapping:* this is the closest existing formalization of the user's distinction. Note the subtle reframe: building the github-workflows skill is *not* toil under this definition (it produces enduring value) even though it *feels* like toil — misclassification of engineering-as-toil is itself a morale hazard.

**2d. Coordination cost (Conway; Mythical Man-Month).** Conway (1967): organizations produce designs mirroring their communication structures (mirroring hypothesis; MIT/HBS evidence cited in support). **[LIKELY]** (full-text verified: `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conway%27s_law`). Brooks's Mythical Man-Month adds communication overhead growing with team size. **[UNVERIFIED in this brief]** (not full-text verified here; cited as background, single-source.) *Mapping:* logistics cost has a coordination component even for solo builders — every public repo must "communicate" with strangers (future self, contributors, users) through README/docs/conventions; that interface cost is real and irreducible, only standardizable.

**2e. Productivity science: SPACE, DORA, DevEx/DX.** (i) The SPACE framework (Forsgren et al.) holds productivity is five-dimensional — Satisfaction/well-being, Performance, Activity, Communication/collaboration, Efficiency/flow — and *cannot* be measured by a single metric. **[LIKELY]** (two independent science-category hits: `https://doi.org/10.1145/3454122.3454124`, `https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11279703/`; abstracts/snippets only, not full-text.) (ii) DORA (Forsgren/Humble/Kim; *Accelerate*, 2018; annual State of DevOps reports, 39,000+ respondents in 2024) measures delivery via four keys — change lead time, deployment frequency, change failure rate, MTTR — over 23,000+ data points, and warns against using team-level metrics for team-by-team evaluation. **[LIKELY]** (full-text verified secondary: `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DevOps_Research_and_Assessment`.) (iii) DevEx (Greiler, Storey, Noda et al.): developer experience = lived experience + friction points; measurement must combine developer feedback with systems data; drives efficiency, quality, retention. **[LIKELY]** (two independent science hits: `https://doi.org/10.1145/3595878`, `https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3595878`; abstracts only.) (iv) The DX Framework (Greiler et al. 2022): semi-structured interviews with 21 industry developers → factors, importance characteristics, improvement strategies, barriers, and *coping mechanisms when DevEx cannot be improved*. **[LIKELY]** (abstract full-text verified: `http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.06352v1`.) *Mapping:* SPACE/DevEx legitimize "fun" as a first-class metric (Satisfaction dimension), and the DX coping-mechanisms finding warns: unfixable friction doesn't disappear, it goes underground.

**2f. Reuse and platforms: IDPs, golden paths, product lines.** (i) Internal Developer Platforms: self-service interfaces that cut cognitive load; separation of concerns between platform teams (standards, golden paths) and app developers (follow the path); claimed effects on deployment frequency, lead time, MTTR, change failure rate. **[UNVERIFIED]** (single community source, full-text verified: `https://internaldeveloperplatform.org/why-build-an-internal-developer-platform/`; its hours-saved table is explicitly asterisked author estimates, not measurements.) (ii) Spotify "golden/paved paths": write the path first, then tooling to shorten it. **[UNVERIFIED]** (SearXNG snippet only: `https://www.infoq.com/news/2021/03/spotify-paved-paths/`; page body login-walled, not extracted.) (iii) Software product-line / reuse literature: systematic reuse beats ad-hoc reuse (small-company study: developers regard reuse as important but do it ad hoc, reducing benefits; need an explicit plan-for-reuse process). **[LIKELY]** (science-category thesis record plus converging SPLE-Scrum interview study, n=49: `https://doi.org/10.3390/electronics12153291`; snippets only.) *Mapping:* the industry already amortizes generic work via platform teams + golden paths; the solo-builder analogue is a *personal platform* (skills, templates, conventions repo) with the same separation of concerns.

**2g. How much time is overhead? (magnitude evidence).** Stripe's Developer Coefficient report (Harris Poll commission) asserts developers as GDP force-multipliers with a $3 trillion / 10-year headline. **[LIKELY for existence + headline]** (landing page full-text verified: `https://stripe.com/newsroom/stories/developer-coefficient`; full report PDF not extracted.) The widely-quoted specifics (≈17 h/week on maintenance, $85B US / $300B global opportunity cost) were **NOT verified** in this session (targeted SearXNG queries returned no citable source) and are therefore **[UNVERIFIED — degraded to snippet lore, do not cite as fact]**. Google's internal 33%-average-toil figure (§2c) is the only full-text-verified magnitude in this brief. Honest summary: every source agrees overhead is *large* (fractions, not percents-of-a-percent); no verified precise number is offered here.

## 3. Top Findings

1. **Generic logistics is accidental complexity + toil-shaped, and the literature says to attack exactly that — but no single attack pays tenfold.** Brooks (accident is removable; no silver bullet) + SRE (toil must be relentlessly eliminated, capped at 50%) converge: logistics deserves engineering investment, but expect cumulative small wins, not one skill/template that fixes everything. **[LIKELY | effect size: direction agreed, magnitude unverified — Brooks claims sub-order-of-magnitude per intervention.]**
2. **The SRE 50% rule is the ready-made division-of-labour policy: cap reactive-generic work, ring-fence ≥50% for engineering (including logistics-engineering).** Google's quarterly-survey mean of ≈33% toil shows the cap is operable. Applied solo: timebox per-project logistics (the cap), and count building cross-project skills/templates as *engineering*, never as toil — misclassification is what makes it feel unfun. **[LIKELY | effect size: 50% cap policy / 33% observed mean (Google-internal, n undisclosed).]**
3. **Not all yaks are waste; the profitable ones are distinguishable in advance.** Kerr's taxonomy (attack vs trim vs golden yaks) plus SRE's toil test (no enduring value + O(n) scaling = toil; permanent improvement = engineering) jointly give a triage rule: shave the yak iff the shave generalizes (≥2 future uses) — otherwise hack through it. **[UNVERIFIED (practitioner sources) | effect size: none quantified — qualitative rule.]**
4. **The industry amortization vehicle is the platform + golden path, and it has a solo-builder form.** IDPs separate platform concerns from product concerns by construction; Spotify writes the path before the tooling; reuse research says ad-hoc reuse underperforms planned reuse. Solo analogue: a personal platform layer (skills like github-workflows, README/docs templates, conventions) maintained *outside* any single project, consumed *inside* every project. **[LIKELY (reuse research) / UNVERIFIED (IDP/golden-path specifics) | effect size: IDP hours-saved table is author-estimated, not measured — treat as directional.]**
5. **Fun is a productivity metric, not a perk — and unfixable friction goes underground.** SPACE's Satisfaction dimension, DevEx's retention/productivity link, and the DX finding on coping mechanisms (developers route around unfixable friction) together imply: a method that is correct but unfun will be silently bypassed, including by its own author. Protecting the specific-work fun is a design constraint, not sentimentality. **[LIKELY | effect size: DevEx paper claims business-performance linkage; numeric effects not extracted — directional only.]**

## 4. Design Principles

P1. **Separate by generalization test, not by feel (the Toil/Engineering triage).** When logistics interrupts specific work, ask two SRE-derived questions: (a) *will the fix outlive this task?* (b) *does this task scale O(n) with projects if left manual?* Yes to either → it is **platform engineering**: stop project work, build/fix it in the personal-platform layer (skills, templates, scripts), then resume. No to both → it is **tactical overhead**: hack through minimally (hard timebox, no polishing) and log it. Rationale: feelings misclassify (building github-workflows *felt* like toil but was engineering); the two questions classify correctly. *Actionable form:* a 2-question gate at every logistics interruption + a per-project logistics timebox (SRE 50% rule, solo-scaled: e.g., logistics must not consume two consecutive work sessions without a platform-layer decision).

P2. **Amortize through a personal platform with golden paths, maintained outside projects.** Keep exactly one cross-project layer (skills repo / conventions / templates / scripts — the github-workflows skill is the first resident) that owns everything passing the P1 generalization test. Rule of capture: every generalized fix lands there the same session it is built (no "I'll extract it later" — reuse research: ad-hoc intent without a plan stays ad hoc). Rule of consumption: new projects start by *inheriting* the platform (README format, docs layout, push conventions), never by re-deriving it. Rule of ordering (Spotify): write the golden path (convention doc) *before* the tooling (skill/script). This converts the user's "half my time is logistics" into "half my time was platform investment with a decaying balance" — each project pays less than the last.

P3. **Protect the specific-work fun as a metric (Satisfaction-gated method).** Track per-project logistics share (rough fraction is enough; SPACE warns against single-metric gaming, so pair it with a 1-line fun/frustration note). If logistics exceeds the cap two projects running, the next session is platform work, not project work — scheduled, not guilt-driven. And keep a G3-style build inside the specific work (attempt-first, artifact-gated) so the fun part stays generative, not managerial. Rationale: DX coping-mechanisms finding — unfelt-unmeasured friction gets routed around, including your own method.

P4. (Supporting) **Standardize the interface, not the interior.** Conway's-law corollary for solo OSS: strangers (and future-you) meet your project only through README/docs/push-hygiene — standardize exactly that surface via the platform, and leave project interiors free. Over-standardizing interiors is how platforms become the new unfun.

P5. (Supporting) **No silver-bullet expectations: budget logistics as permanent, shrinking accident.** Per Brooks, each platform investment yields sub-order-of-magnitude returns; the win is cumulative and asymptotic, never zero-logistics. A method promising to eliminate logistics entirely will be experienced as failure; a method promising each project is cheaper than the last will be experienced as progress.

## 5. Not-to-Encode

1. **Do NOT encode "zero logistics" as a goal or promise.** Brooks: accident shrinks but essence remains; coordination interfaces (README/docs for strangers) are irreducible. A zero-logistics target guarantees perceived failure. (Encode P5's decaying-balance framing instead.)
2. **Do NOT encode project-interior standards** (code layout, architecture choices, algorithm style) into the cross-project layer. Evidence: golden paths cover the *path to production*, not the product (Spotify/IDP, UNVERIFIED but consistent); over-standardization raises cognitive load, the opposite of the platform's purpose.
3. **Do NOT encode human-judgment work as automatable toil.** SRE is explicit: if human judgment is essential, it is probably not toil. README *content* (the whys), ADR decisions, docs-of-intent stay human; only their *format/pipeline* (templates, checks, push mechanics) gets tooled.
4. **Do NOT encode a single productivity number as success** (e.g., "logistics under X%"). SPACE (multi-dimensionality) + DORA's own warning against team-level metric misuse both forbid it. Pair any share-metric with the satisfaction note (P3).
5. **Do NOT encode yak-shaving detection as suppression.** Kerr: trim and golden yaks are investments; a method that punishes all deviation from the plan kills exactly the generalizations that amortize. Encode the P1 triage, not a ban.
6. **Do NOT encode the unverified magnitude numbers** (Stripe ~17 h/week, $85B; IDP hours-saved table; Spotify path effects) as facts in any downstream method doc. They are snippet-lore or author estimates until verified. Cite only the verified claims in §2–3.
7. **Do NOT encode platform-building as unallocated guilt work.** If it isn't scheduled (P3's platform session rule) and counted as engineering (P1/SRE classification), it will be experienced as toil regardless of its true category — the DX coping-mechanisms finding predicts the method gets routed around.

## 6. Open Gaps

1. **Verified magnitude for solo/small-team OSS builders.** All full-text-verified numbers here are Google-internal (33% toil) or enterprise survey aggregates (DORA). No verified study quantifies logistics share for solo multi-repo OSS builders with agent assistance. Next query: Stack Overflow Developer Survey + JetBrains State of Developer Ecosystem time-use items; Tidelift maintainer surveys.
2. **Unverified specifics carried as lore.** Stripe maintenance-hours/$85B figures; IDP hours-saved table; Spotify golden-path effects; SPACE/DevEx numeric effect sizes. Each needs full-text verification (report PDFs, ACM full text) before use in any method doc.
3. **Amortization accounting.** No source found gives a rule for *when* a generalization has paid off (how many reuses justify what platform investment). SRE's 50% cap is a rate limit, not an ROI rule. Open: a lightweight ledger (cost-to-build vs sessions-saved) for the personal platform.
4. **Agent-specific division of labour.** The literature predates agent-assisted solo building: which side (generic vs specific) should agents own? Hypothesis from §2c: agents absorb O(n)-scaling toil first (push mechanics, format checks), humans keep judgment (whys, ADRs) — untested, needs a small N-of-1 experiment across the author's own projects.
5. **Fun as leading indicator.** SPACE/DevEx establish satisfaction↔productivity linkage at org scale; no verified instrument for solo-builder session-level fun/frustration tracking. P3's 1-line note is a placeholder awaiting a real micro-instrument.
