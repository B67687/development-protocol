## 1. Problem Statement

The user asks two coupled questions (verbatim): (T-082) "how generalistic does dev protocol apply to any kind of project? … projects at different kinds of development stages, greenfield, brownfield, whatever"; (T-083) "how good is it at continuation on itself? or like picking up a brownfield project vs greenfield vs other kinds of projects of different starting points already."

Restated as a research question: **what is the right generality/tailoring trade-off for a single development method across project types × lifecycle stages, and what concrete continuation mechanisms does the evidence support for each starting point (greenfield / brownfield / legacy / stalled-self)?** The answer must avoid two failure modes: (a) a method so generic it gives no purchase on any concrete situation, and (b) a method so tailored per situation that "continuation" becomes re-invention every time. The thesis defended below: **stable kernel + situational shell + explicit continuation protocol** — a small non-negotiable core that is identical everywhere, a configured periphery selected per contingency factors, and a first-class resume/handover procedure that is the same mechanism whether the predecessor was a human, a past session, or the method itself.

## 2. Field Landscape

Seven sub-fields bear on the question; each is mapped to what it contributes and what it cannot settle.

**2.1. Situational Method Engineering (SME) and method tailoring.** The foundational discipline: methods should be *constructed per situation* from reusable fragments stored in a method base, balancing standardisation against fit ("controlled flexibility") — Harmsen's thesis formulation [CONFIRMED — definitional, 2+ sources agree] (https://doi.org/10.3990/1.9789075498103; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Method_engineering). The SME state-of-the-art review surveys metamodels, fragment/chunk identification, and construction processes including the "map" vs. "deontic matrix" techniques, closing with method tailoring and quality evaluation as open research [LIKELY — single authoritative survey] (https://zenodo.org/record/7001089). The Wikipedia synthesis adds the operational definition of tailoring: "responsive changes in, and dynamic interplays between contexts, intentions, and method fragments determine a system development approach for a specific project situation" (Aydin), plus the claim that nearly all agile methods are suitable for tailoring and that situation-appropriateness distinguishes agile from rigid plan-driven methods [LIKELY — encyclopedic, cites Abrahamsson/Aydin] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Method_engineering). An evaluation-framework paper further finds existing SME methods lose tacit knowledge (method-engineer experience not captured) and proposes KM support assessed over eight SME methods and four industrial cases [LIKELY — single peer-reviewed source] (https://doi.org/10.3897/jucs.110894).

**2.2. Contingency / "one size does not fit all" evidence.** Project Contingency Theory work elicited **28 project contingency factors** from SLR + manager interviews as the selection basis for situational project-management method engineering [LIKELY — single peer-reviewed source, grounded in both theory and practice] (http://aisel.aisnet.org/irwitpm2011/6). The Motorola inductive study documents sophisticated **multi-level tailoring (industry → organisation → project)** that sidesteps per-project lengthy tailoring — the practical answer to "tailoring is too expensive" [LIKELY — single field study] (http://aisel.aisnet.org/ecis2000/4). Agile-practice selection theses converge on the same verdict: selection by personal preference/past experience dominates in practice and is inadequate; project properties must drive practice choice; no single methodology fits all industries so "methodology tailoring and careful assessment of the relevant factors is warranted" [LIKELY — thesis + SLR convergence, community-tier venues] (http://hdl.handle.net/11073/5902; http://lup.lub.lu.se/student-papers/record/9018035). The TARGET framework (SLR: **95 adapted practices** from five scaled-agile frameworks across 17 business domains, 2007–2021) confirms large-scale distributed teams *routinely* tailor rather than adopt verbatim [LIKELY — single SLR] (https://repositorio.ufpe.br/handle/123456789/52437). A "family of methodologies / practices, not methodologies" argument (Wits thesis) makes the same architectural point: select practices per project, not labels [UNVERIFIED — single thesis, community tier] (http://hdl.handle.net/10539/5666).

**2.3. Greenfield vs. brownfield vs. legacy modernisation.** Brownfield = development in the presence of existing systems/constraints; the practitioner consensus is that **most real work is brownfield** ("modern custom software development rarely starts from scratch") [UNVERIFIED — practitioner claims, no effect size] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brownfield_(software_development)). The authoritative modernisation pattern is Fowler's **Strangler Fig**: gradual replacement beats big-bang rewrites, which "go down in flames most of the time"; four activities — (1) clarify outcomes, (2) break into parts via seams, (3) deliver parts for early return, (4) change the organisation (Conway) — plus explicit **transitional architecture** that is thrown away [CONFIRMED — practitioner-authoritative + peer-reviewed case-study convergence] (https://martinfowler.com/bliki/StranglerFigApplication.html; https://www.ijcesen.com/index.php/ijcesen/article/view/4965). Supporting evidence: an SRE-in-brownfield-retail multi-case study (3 orgs, 30 interviews) finds barriers are **cultural rather than technical** (blameless postmortems, error budgets, SLO definition for monoliths) and that SRE metrics supply the objective language for prioritising technical debt [LIKELY — single qualitative multi-case study] (https://aimjournals.com/article-analytics/go/pdf.php?u=https%3A%2F%2Faimjournals.com%2Findex.php%2Fijmcsit%2Farticle%2Fview%2F351%2F323&j=ijmcsit&a=351&g=323). An architecture-centric microservices-migration methodology paper adds that academia produced many techniques but "practitioners hardly leverage these resources" — a transfer gap, not an evidence gap [LIKELY — single paper] (https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-48550-3_5).

**2.4. Onboarding / takeover / program comprehension (the cost of starting mid-stream).** Developers spend **58–70% of their time on code comprehension** [LIKELY — thesis synthesising prior studies; widely cited range, single-source trace here] (https://repositorio.ufpe.br/handle/123456789/56900). Over **42% of 2,401 reviewed code-review comments target understandability**, 83.9% accepted, <1% reverted — understandability work sticks [LIKELY — single TSE study] (https://doi.org/10.1109/tse.2024.3453783). The Microsoft onboarding case study (32 developers + 15 managers interviewed; triangulated with N=189 developer + N=37 manager surveys) finds onboarding is mostly engineering tasks (bug fixes, small features) operating through **learning / confidence-building / socialisation**, with three manager strategies used "unknowingly" needing *situational* recommendations — the same contingency moral at team scale [CONFIRMED — peer-reviewed ICSE'21 case study with survey triangulation] (http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.05055). OpenStack-ecosystem onboarding-vs-productivity evidence exists but was abstract-only in this sweep [UNVERIFIED here] (https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9402037/).

**2.5. Knowledge concentration, handover, and self-continuation decay.** Truck/bus-factor tooling (Knowledge Islands) operationalises the risk that departure of key developers makes continuation impossible [LIKELY — single tool paper] (http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08733). OSS longitudinal data (18 orgs on GitHub): **all core developers take breaks**; ~45% disengage ≥1 year; pause→return probability **~35–55%**, but after a year-long break resume probability drops to **~21–26%** with ~54% chance of full disengagement [LIKELY — single longitudinal study, strong N but one platform] (https://doi.org/10.1007/s10664-021-10012-6). A stochastic KLRM simulation claims full lever activation (ADRs, pair programming, LLM-assisted development) raises expected knowledge capital **+63.8%** and "virtually eliminates" crisis probability — treat as an upper-bound illustration, not an empirical effect [LIKELY at most — PREPRINT simulation, no field validation] (https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23257).

**2.6. Lifecycle/stage-gate applicability.** Stage-gate and SDLC-phase thinking survives inside the agile-selection literature (teaching case: methodology choice as multi-criteria decision over project characteristics; SLR classifying method changes by SDLC locus) [UNVERIFIED — pedagogical/SLR sources, no causal effects] (http://aisel.aisnet.org/jmwais/vol1/iss1/4; https://doi.org/10.4236/jcc.2017.55007). No source in this sweep gives stage-contingent *effect sizes* for method choice; the gap is recorded in §6.

**2.7. What the sweep did NOT find.** No empirical study of "a method continuing *itself*" (self-resumption of an agent/method across sessions) in the peer-reviewed record; the continuation evidence is all human-team handover/onboarding. Agent-session continuation must therefore be designed by analogy (ADRs ≈ decision memory; method base ≈ fragment memory; gates ≈ attention checks), with confidence capped accordingly.

## 3. Top Findings

1. **Controlled flexibility is the validated generality posture: one kernel, many configurations.** SME's core result — rigid standard methods fail situational fit; pure ad-hoc tuning fails repeatability; the optimum is constructing project-specific methods from a shared fragment repository — is the direct answer to "how generalistic" [CONFIRMED; no numeric effect size available] (https://doi.org/10.3990/1.9789075498103; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Method_engineering; https://zenodo.org/record/7001089).
2. **Comprehension dominates takeover cost: 58–70% of developer time; 42%+ of review effort is understandability work.** Continuation is therefore primarily a *comprehension* problem, not a process problem — any resume protocol that does not budget for and accelerate program comprehension will underperform regardless of ceremony [LIKELY; ranges from thesis synthesis + TSE study, not RCTs] (https://repositorio.ufpe.br/handle/123456789/56900; https://doi.org/10.1109/tse.2024.3453783).
3. **Incremental strangler-style continuation beats big-bang restart — technically, economically, and organisationally.** Rewrite-from-scratch "goes down in flames most of the time" (Fowler); incremental patterns show defect/incident deltas, early ROI per slice, and preserved institutional knowledge; the main blockers are cultural (blamelessness, error budgets, Conway alignment), and transitional scaffolding costs are outweighed by risk reduction [CONFIRMED at pattern level; case-study effect sizes only] (https://martinfowler.com/bliki/StranglerFigApplication.html; https://www.ijcesen.com/index.php/ijcesen/article/view/4965; https://aimjournals.com/article-analytics/go/pdf.php?u=https%3A%2F%2Faimjournals.com%2Findex.php%2Fijmcsit%2Farticle%2Fview%2F351%2F323&j=ijmcsit&a=351&g=323).
4. **Onboarding/continuation quality is task-mediated and situational: small tasks → learning/confidence/socialisation; strategies must match the newcomer.** Microsoft ICSE'21 (32+15 interviews, N=189+37 surveys) plus the 28-contingency-factor catalogue jointly imply a continuation protocol cannot be one script — it needs task ladders (bug → feature → slice) and manager-strategy selection per situation [CONFIRMED for task-mediation; LIKELY for the 28-factor catalogue's completeness] (http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.05055; http://aisel.aisnet.org/irwitpm2011/6).
5. **Interruption decays return probability steeply; multi-level pre-tailoring is the cost control.** After a year-long break, resume odds fall to ~21–26% (OSS data); the Motorola answer to tailoring cost is to pre-tailor at industry/organisation level so per-project resumption is cheap selection, not fresh design [LIKELY; single-platform longitudinal + single-org field study] (https://doi.org/10.1007/s10664-021-10012-6; http://aisel.aisnet.org/ecis2000/4). Corollary for self-continuation: a method that cannot cheaply re-derive its own configured state after interruption will exhibit the same decay curve as a departed maintainer.

## 4. Design Principles

**P1 — Kernel/shell split: freeze the invariants, configure the rest.** Encode a ≤7-item kernel that is identical at every starting point (e.g., outcome-first scoping; smallest shippable slice; written contrast/prediction-vs-actual; immediate specific feedback; decision log; done-gate; retrospect-and-store). Everything else — iteration length, ceremony weight, review formality, modernisation pattern, onboarding task ladder — is selected from a fragment catalogue via explicit contingency factors (start from the 28-factor list, prune to ~8 project-salient ones: codebase age/size, team familiarity, delivery pressure, regulatory load, architectural seam availability, test coverage, turnover risk, outcome clarity). *Why:* SME + contingency literature jointly warrant this and nothing else with CONFIRMED-level support (§3.1, §3.4).

**P2 — Continuation is comprehension-first: every resume opens with a comprehension slice, never with process.** Mandate the resume sequence: (i) map state (what exists, what runs, what the last verified checkpoint was); (ii) one comprehension artifact (trace, seam map, or ADR backfill) before any feature work; (iii) smallest behavioral slice that exercises the comprehension claim. Budget it explicitly (≈ half the first session, per the 58–70% finding). *Why:* takeover cost is dominated by understanding; review-comment data shows understandability fixes stick (83.9% acceptance), so comprehension artifacts are durable capital, not overhead (§3.2).

**P3 — Strangler + transitional scaffolding as the default brownfield/legacy/self-continuation move.** New work grows alongside the old (legacy code, stale docs, or the method's own outdated plan); seams are identified; slices are displaced one at a time; throwaway transitional architecture (adapters, shims, migration ledgers) is explicitly permitted and scheduled for removal; Conway alignment (who owns the new slice) is decided per slice. Greenfield is the special case (empty host), not the default — the same mechanism with zero legacy routing. *Why:* big-bang restart evidence is negative; incremental displacement has the only positive case evidence in the sweep (§3.3).

**P4 — Cheap re-derivation: pre-tailor at org level, persist decision memory, gate resumption.** Maintain (a) an organisation-level default configuration (Motorola-style, so resumption is selection not design); (b) a decision log (ADRs or equivalent — the KLRM "knowledge lever" with the strongest mechanistic story); (c) a verifiable last-known-good checkpoint (tests + artifact equality, cf. G3's triple-equality pattern) that a resuming agent/human replays before trusting inherited state. Treat any resume after long interruption as onboarding (task ladder per P2 + Microsoft strategies), not as "reading the docs." *Why:* interruption decay is steep and measured; tacit-knowledge loss is SME's documented weakness (§3.5; https://doi.org/10.3897/jucs.110894).

## 5. Not-to-Encode

1. **Do NOT encode "one method verbatim for all projects."** Directly refuted by the contingency/tailoring convergence (28 factors; 95 adapted practices; no-fits-all teaching-case verdict). A rigid universal script is the failure mode SME was invented to escape.
2. **Do NOT encode "re-derive the method fresh per project."** Refuted by cost evidence (Motorola multi-level tailoring exists precisely because per-project full tailoring is unaffordable). Encode selection-from-catalogue, not design-from-scratch.
3. **Do NOT encode big-bang rewrite/restart as a continuation strategy** (for legacy code *or* for the method's own stale state). Fowler's negative verdict + incremental case evidence make this the dominated option; allow only with written justification and risk acceptance.
4. **Do NOT encode comprehension as "read the docs/code" without a produced artifact.** The comprehension literature + G3-adjacent reflection criterion require generated, checkable product (trace, seam map, ADR); passive re-reading inherits no warrant.
5. **Do NOT encode the +63.8% knowledge-capital figure (or any precise ROI) as a promise.** It is a PREPRINT Monte-Carlo illustration, not a field effect; citing it as expected value would be fabrication-adjacent. Cap at "mechanistically plausible, empirically unvalidated."
6. **Do NOT encode greenfield as the default starting point.** The practitioner consensus (most work is brownfield) plus the decay data imply the resume/continuation path is the common case; greenfield is the boundary condition.
7. **Do NOT encode agent self-continuation as empirically validated.** No peer-reviewed source in this sweep studies method/agent self-resumption; all continuation evidence is human-team analogical. Mark self-continuation mechanisms PROVISIONAL pending the probe in §6.5.

## 6. Open Gaps

1. **Stage-contingent effect sizes.** No source gives causal effects of method choice *by lifecycle stage* (inception vs. growth vs. maturity vs. sunset). Best available is multi-criteria-selection pedagogy. Next query: `SearXNG science: "software development methodology" project stage contingency performance outcomes`.
2. **Greenfield-vs-brownfield productivity differentials.** The brownfield-dominance claim is practitioner consensus without a measured productivity delta. Next query: `SearXNG science: brownfield greenfield developer productivity comparative empirical`.
3. **Strangler-fig controlled evidence.** Support is authoritative-practitioner + case studies with defect/incident deltas; no RCT or matched-cohort comparison vs. big-bang exists (and may be infeasible). Record as structural gap, not just missing search.
4. **Handover-protocol experiments.** Onboarding evidence is observational/case-study; no trials compare resume protocols (checklist vs. task ladder vs. pair-resume) on time-to-productive. Next query: `SearXNG science: software team handover protocol intervention time-to-productivity`.
5. **Self-continuation probe (method resuming itself).** Entirely absent from the record. Proposed internal probe: interrupt a configured session mid-slice, resume from checkpoint + decision log only, and measure slices-to-productive vs. cold restart — the agent analogue of the OSS 35–55% / 21–26% decay curve. This is the T-083 question in directly testable form.
