# LANDSCAPE Research: Controllable-Proxy X-Y (THOUGHT T-059)

- **Date:** 2026-10-03
- **Thought:** T-059 — controllable-proxy X-Y
- **Core pattern:** A user states a controllable auxiliary problem X hoping it fixes a real anxiety Y; stated X vs tacit Y. Canonical example: Chinese specialty/major choice (X = "which major should I pick") masking real anxiety (Y = parental pressure, gaokao aftermath, identity, autonomy).
- **Method:** SearXNG self-hosted (127.0.0.1:8888, categories=science + general follow-ups), page extraction attempted (bot-blocked; snippets only), confidence labels per claim.
- **Confidence scheme:** CONFIRMED = replicated/meta-analytic or canonical primary source; LIKELY = single-study or strong convergent theory; UNVERIFIED = plausible, needs verification.

## 1. Problem Statement

T-059 observes a recurring help-seeking shape: the user presents X, a concrete, controllable, solvable-adjacent problem ("help me choose a specialty"), while the actual distress driver Y is vaguer, less controllable, and often unsaid (family pressure, fear of disappointing parents, post-gaokao depression onset, autonomy loss). Solving X as stated risks (a) failing to relieve Y, (b) legitimizing avoidance of Y, (c) producing advice that is technically correct but humanly wrong (e.g., optimizing major choice while the student is clinically depressed). The research question: what does the literature say about stated-vs-tacit problems, control-seeking proxies, and how should a thinking agent handle X-without-Y?

## 3. Top Findings (with confidence + effect sizes)

**F1. Stigma suppresses help-seeking for the real problem — so Y arrives disguised as X. (CONFIRMED, d ≈ small-to-moderate)**
Clement et al. 2015 systematic review (located via PubMed ID 24569086, search-returned URL https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24569086/) finds mental-health stigma has a small-to-moderate negative effect on help-seeking. Implication for T-059: when Y is stigmatized (anxiety, depression, family conflict), users rationally repackage it as a legitimate instrumental X ("which specialty"). The disguise is adaptive self-protection, not deception — the agent must not punish or expose it bluntly.

**F2. Loss of control drives compensatory proxy-seeking: people fixate on controllable X when uncontrollable Y threatens them. (CONFIRMED theory; LIKELY mechanism for T-059)**
Compensatory Control Theory (Kay and colleagues; chapter anchor https://www.taylorfrancis.com/chapters/edit/10.4324/9781315661452-12/compensatory-control-theory-psychological-importance-perceiving-order-bastiaan-rutjens-aaron-kay): when personal control drops, people affirm external structure/order. Convergent: the Chorpita & Barlow model positions diminished perceived control as central to anxiety etiology, and panic/GAD patients show lower perceived control than healthy controls (search-returned study, https://doi.org/10.1007/s12144-022-03842-0). For T-059 this reframes X-fixation as a control-restoration strategy: choosing a major is controllable; confronting parents or an uncertain identity is not. Ripping X away without replacing the control function spikes anxiety.

**F3. Strategy must fit perceived controllability — mismatched "just solve X" advice backfires under low control. (LIKELY, diary-study evidence)**
A 7-day diary study (n=75 undergraduates; https://doi.org/10.1007/s42761-024-00275-9) found strategy–situation fit predicts depressed mood: emotion-focused coping helps under high-stress/low-control conditions but harms under high-stress/high-control ones, and vice versa for problem-focused coping. T-059 corollary: if Y is low-control (family system, gaokao already taken), pure problem-solving on X is the wrong prescription; emotion-focused/acceptance work on Y fits better. Encoding "always reframe to Y" is equally wrong — fit depends on assessed controllability.

**F4. Cognitive reframing works (SMD anxiety −0.21, depression −0.66, stress −0.23) but does not change coping/burden appraisals alone. (CONFIRMED, Cochrane)**
Cochrane review of cognitive reframing for dementia carers (https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.cd005318.pub2): pooled SMD −0.21 anxiety, −0.66 depression, −0.23 subjective stress; no effect on coping appraisal, burden appraisal, or institutionalization. T-059 corollary: reframing X→Y can relieve symptoms with medium effects on depression, but reframing alone does not change the user's coping repertoire — the agent must pair any reframe with concrete coping support, ideally multi-component and tailored ("impact higher when used alongside other interventions").

**F5. The gaokao-to-college window concentrates new-onset depression (9-month incidence 2.3%), with ~36% of depressed youth debuting in that window — while treatment reaches <9%. (CONFIRMED single large study)**
Hunan Normal University two-stage epidemiological survey (N=6,818 screened, 926 interviewed with K-SADS-PL; https://doi.org/10.1186/s12888-023-04972-w): sex-adjusted 9-month incidence of new-onset depressive disorders 2.3% (comparable to the global annual ~3.0% compressed into 9 months); over one-third (36.5%) of depressed youth had onset in the gaokao-to-matriculation window; median onset age 17; adjusted lifetime treatment rate only 8.7%. Risk factors: maternal higher education, major life events, female sex, parental divorce/death. T-059 corollary: the Chinese-specialty-choice example sits inside a statistically dense depression-onset window — a "which major" query in June–October is a priori higher-risk for masked Y, and professional-help referral base rates are extremely low, so the agent is often the only accessible listener.

**Supporting (LIKELY/UNVERIFIED):**
- S1. The XY Problem as named by Meta Stack Exchange (https://meta.stackexchange.com/questions/66377/what-is-the-xy-problem): user asks about attempted solution rather than actual problem — CONFIRMED as a stable community construct (2010, still canonical), UNVERIFIED as an empirically measured frequency. T-059 is its affective twin: engineering XY is about missing information; proxy X-Y is about missing safety to name Y.
- S2. Client reactions predict counselling outcomes and counsellors adjust strategies mid-stream (http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15334v1) — LIKELY; supports designing T-059 handling as a feedback loop (probe → read reaction → adjust), not a one-shot reframe.
- S3. Parental academic pressure + acculturation predicts anxiety in Chinese international students (APA record https://doi.apa.org/doi/10.1037/e540862014-001; full text paywalled — UNVERIFIED details, LIKELY direction).
- S4. Gaokao arts/science subject-choice inflexibility and rural–urban enrolment gaps (https://doi.org/10.54097/awwh7165) — LIKELY structural backdrop: X ("which track") is partly externally constrained, so advice assuming free choice misfires.

## 4. Design Principles for T-059 (encode these)

**P1. Honor X before touching Y (alliance first, reframe second).**
MI-consistent autonomy support + presenting-problem ethics: solve/audit X competently and briefly first. Only then, with earned licence, name Y tentatively ("I can help with the major list — and I notice a lot of the weight seems to be about your parents' reaction; want to talk about that part too?"). Never lead with the reframe; never refuse X to force Y.

**P2. Assess controllability, then match the strategy (fit, not reflex).**
Ask one discriminating question: is the distress driver changeable by the user (high control → problem-solve X hard) or systemic/past (low control → emotion-focused work on Y: naming, normalizing, coping, referral)? Encode the diary-study fit rule (F3), not a blanket "always dig for Y."

**P3. Treat X-fixation as control restoration, and replace the function before removing the form.**
If the user clings to X, that clinging is doing anxiety-work (F2). Any move toward Y must hand back equal-or-greater agency: small controllable next steps inside Y ("one sentence you could say to your mother"), not just insight. Pair every reframe with a coping action (F4: reframing alone doesn't change coping).

## 5. Not-to-Encode (explicit non-goals)

- **N1. Do not diagnose.** No depression/anxiety labels from chat text, even in the gaokao window (F5 raises prior, never licenses diagnosis). Referral language only, no severity claims.
- **N2. Do not expose or shame the disguise.** Never say "your real problem is…" as fact, never imply dishonesty (F1: disguise is stigma-rational). Tentative, permission-based naming only.
- **N3. Do not cultural-stereotype.** The Chinese-specialty example must not become "Chinese users really mean family pressure." Parental-pressure findings (S3/S4) are population-level; apply by listening, not by profiling.
- **N4. Do not always reframe.** Blanket X→Y reframing violates strategy–situation fit (F3) and risks breaking alliance. Sometimes X is just X — solve it and stop.

## 6. Open Gaps

- **G1.** No direct empirical literature on "controllable-proxy" help-seeking as a named phenomenon — the construct is synthesized here from adjacent fields; needs a targeted search (terms: "displaced help-seeking", "proxy concern", "instrumental presenting problem") or primary interview work.
- **G2.** Effect of agent-delivered X→Y reframing on alliance/retention: counselling evidence is human-therapist; LLM-chatbot transfer unmeasured.
- **G3.** S3 full text (parental academic pressure × acculturation) unverified — paywalled APA record; retrieve via institutional access.
- **G4.** Connector/extractor coverage: page extraction was bot-blocked (403/cookie walls) for Meta.SE and PubMed; only search snippets verified. Re-run via the 127.0.0.1:8081 extractor sidecar or institutional access before promoting S1–S4 above LIKELY.
